# Infectious Disease Dynamics Tool Communities

Data underlying the "IDD tools" section of the Global Society for Infectious Disease Dynamics ([gsidd.org](https://www.gsidd.org)) website. This file lists **communities of practice** — networks, consortia, centres, hubs and open-source organisations — that are organised around developing, hosting or curating infectious disease dynamics (IDD) **software tools**. The test applied is whether the community's identity is built around software: a research consortium that happens to have produced a model is not included, while a group whose shared output is a tool, a package suite, a hub standard or a curated toolchain is. It is a companion to [database_tools.md](database_tools.md) (the tools themselves) and [database_ecosystems.md](database_ecosystems.md) (interoperable tool ecosystems).

## Schema

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| Name | Common name, with acronym expansion where the organisation itself uses one |
| Type | Organisational form — see vocabulary below |
| Region / scope | Geographic reach |
| Focus | Pathogens, diseases or methods the community concentrates on |
| Host / funder | Institution hosting the secretariat and, where verified, the principal funder |
| Since | Year the community was established, where verifiable from a primary source |
| Status | Whether the community appears currently active, and any caveat |
| Description | One factual sentence |
| Website | Current official URL (all checked to return HTTP 200 on 2026-08-07 unless noted) |

**Type vocabulary.** *Consortium* — a formally constituted multi-institution collaboration with a secretariat. *Network* — a membership network of individuals or groups, usually with open or semi-open membership. *Community of practice* — a self-organising membership community, typically without a single host institution. *Open-source community* — an organisation or informal group whose primary output is shared software. *Modelling hub* — a coordinated multi-team forecasting or scenario-projection effort. *Research centre* — a single-institution centre or institute. *Agency programme* — a programme run by a national or international public health agency. *Society subgroup* — a thematic subgroup of a learned society.

**Status vocabulary.** *Active* — dated evidence of activity in 2025–2026. *Active, with caveat* — the organisation appears to be operating but its public web presence is stale or partly broken; the caveat states what was observed. Communities that have wound down, or for which no dated evidence of recent activity could be found, are not listed; they are recorded in [gsidd_orgs_suggestions.md](gsidd_orgs_suggestions.md) or dropped entirely.

## Methodology and caveats

**Snapshot date: 2026-08-07.** Membership, funding, hosting arrangements and website addresses in this field change frequently; treat every field as a point-in-time observation.

**How this list was built.** Candidates were seeded from the eight examples in [details.md](details.md) and then extended by systematic web search across several categories: research consortia and modelling networks; funder-convened groups; national and regional public health modelling networks; scenario and forecast hub consortia; open-source community organisations; disease-specific modelling consortia (malaria, TB, HIV, polio, measles, HPV, NTDs, cholera, rabies, dengue, AMR, hepatitis); training and capacity networks; and regional networks in low- and middle-income countries. Each candidate was then checked against its own website, and, where the site was uninformative, against institutional pages, grant records and its public code repositories. Every URL in the table above was fetched with `curl` following redirects and returned HTTP 200 on the snapshot date.

**Scope decisions.** Three categories were deliberately excluded. *Training and capacity-building organisations* (ICI3D, SISMID, Applied Epi) are out, because general training belongs elsewhere on gsidd.org rather than in the tools section. *Adjacent research consortia* whose subject is not IDD software (CoMSES Net, GRAM, the Malaria Atlas Project, Polaris Observatory, COR-NTD) are out. *Wound-down organisations* are out, including SPECTRUM and Epiverse-TRACE LAC, whose funded periods have ended. Forecast and scenario hubs are treated as communities and listed here rather than as ecosystems, even where they also ship an interoperable package suite; hubverse is the main case and appears in both files for that reason.

**Overlap with the GSIDD "IDD Orgs" page.** GSIDD already publishes an [IDD organization landscape](https://www.gsidd.org/idd-orgs) page. That list and this file were built independently; two entries here (AM2NTD and CEMA) were found via that page and then verified separately, as was the African Society for Biomathematics, which is listed on that page but is not a software community and so is not repeated here. Organisations that are genuine IDD bodies but are not software communities have been collected in [gsidd_orgs_suggestions.md](gsidd_orgs_suggestions.md) as candidates for that directory instead.

**Fields left as `—`.** Where a founding year, host institution or funder could not be confirmed from a primary source it is recorded as `—` rather than inferred. Notably: CANMOD's website never expands its own acronym; AMMnet, Epinowcast and Mosqlimate do not state their funders publicly; and several research centres do not publish a founding date.

**Sites that block automated requests.** `cdc.gov` and `lshtm.ac.uk` return HTTP 403 to scripted requests regardless of headers. For those organisations the table uses an equivalent canonical URL that does resolve: `insightnet.us` for CDC's Insight Net, and `cmmid.github.io` for LSHTM's CMMID. The US CDC Center for Forecasting and Outbreak Analytics is therefore represented in the Host/funder column rather than as its own row.

**Candidates that were checked and excluded.** The following were investigated and rejected, mostly because they are defunct, or because they are real and active but do not develop, host or curate IDD software: the Malaria Modeling Consortium (domain now a parking page; IHME secretariat page removed); SCORE, the Schistosomiasis Consortium for Operational Research and Evaluation (its own site states activities completed September 2020); the Rockefeller Foundation Pandemic Prevention Institute (wound down September 2022); the European COVID-19 Forecast Hub and European COVID-19 Scenario Hub (both domains no longer resolve; superseded by RespiCast and RespiCompass); the US COVID-19 Forecast Hub run by the Reich Lab (stopped accepting submissions May 2024, now an archive, with the live hub moved to CDC); OMNI-RÉUNIS, the Canadian One Health Modelling Network for Emerging Infections (`omni-reunis.ca` no longer resolves); the WHO Rabies Modelling Consortium (a literature authorship collective with no website); the Canine Rabies Blueprint and the PRIME HPV tool site (domains lapsed); the CDC Dengue Forecasting Project (site returns 404); GDAC, the Global Dengue and Aedes-Transmitted Diseases Consortium (no content after October 2023); SEDRIC (content frozen since 2022); and MESA, the Global Task Force on Cholera Control, the WHO Global Arbovirus Initiative, the Measles & Rubella Partnership, United Against Rabies and the Global Alliance for Rabies Control (all active, but as research-tracking, coordination or advocacy bodies rather than tool communities).

**What is likely under-represented.** This survey was conducted in English using English-language search engines, so networks that operate primarily in French, Spanish, Portuguese, Arabic, Russian, Mandarin, Japanese or an African language are systematically harder to find and are almost certainly under-counted. LMIC networks are the clearest gap: the entries here for Africa, South Asia, Southeast Asia and Latin America (SACEMA, MASHA, CEMA, AM2NTD, AMMnet, NDMC, MIDSEA, CERM, MIDAS Latin America, Mosqlimate) were found via English-language sources and are unlikely to be the full set. Several plausible categories returned nothing verifiable at all: no Africa CDC or PAHO convened modelling group could be identified, and apart from the Brazilian Mosqlimate effort no national or regional modelling network was found for Latin America, the Middle East, Eastern Europe or most of East Asia. National modelling networks in France, Italy, Spain, the Nordic countries, Japan and China were searched for without result, which reflects the limits of the search rather than an absence of such groups. Small networks that exist only as a mailing list, a university page or a social media presence will also be missing, as will modelling groups convened inside government with no public web presence — the UK's SPI-M and UKHSA in-house modelling arrangements are an example that could not be pinned to a stable public URL. Additions and corrections are welcome.

**Conflicts of interest.** This list was compiled by Cliff Kerr, who works at the Institute for Disease Modeling, one of the listed communities, and who has collaborated with several others. Entries are sorted alphabetically; the ordering implies no ranking.
